Charge What It's Worth

How to Price Your Sets Without Underselling

Nearly every newcomer opens too cheap. The ranges above are a place to start testing — then move with your own signal: views, purchases, repeat orders, and the time each set eats.

How to set it up

  • Open near the bottom of the range to bank early reviews, then climb as demand shows up.
  • Price bundles at roughly two-thirds of the à-la-carte total to lift average order size.
  • Bill customs at 2–3× and take payment before a single frame is shot.
  • Add a subscription to convert one-off buyers into monthly income.
  • Look after regulars with perks instead of blanket discounts.

Pricing mistakes that cost you money

  • Racing to the bottom to "compete" — it just trains buyers to expect cheap.
  • Never raising prices, even after the reviews and demand are clearly there.
  • Giving away customs at standard rates instead of charging the premium they earn.
  • Ignoring your effective hourly return and mistaking gross sales for profit.
These are testing ideas, not fixed market rates. Your own numbers are the real guide — track what buyers actually pay for and price toward a fair return on your time.
Protect Yourself

How to Sell Feet Pics Safely and Stay Anonymous

The most common horror stories from new sellers are scams — and almost all of them rhyme. A single rule blocks the majority: money first, content second, and never leave the platform.

Block these on sight

  • "I'll pay once you send it" — they take the content and disappear.
  • Screenshots of a "payment" — a picture isn't money in your account.
  • Requests for a "verification" or "unlock" fee — no real buyer asks you to pay.
  • Being pushed onto Cash App, PayPal friends-and-family, or Telegram to dodge protection.
  • "Send a freebie to prove it's really you" — a classic vanish.

Keep your identity locked down

  • Use a stage name with no link to your real accounts.
  • Stay faceless and scrub the frame of anything identifying.
  • Strip location data from files and watermark your previews.
  • Turn on regional blocking where offered to hide your home area.
  • Run a separate inbox and dedicated socials, never your personal ones.

A quick reality check on "anonymous": hiding your face keeps you private to buyers, but you're never invisible to the marketplace, your bank, or the tax office — a verified service still holds your true identity, payout, and tax details. Buyer-facing privacy and being off the grid are not the same thing.

Is It Legal to Sell Feet Pics — and Is It Taxable?

For adults 18 and up, selling your own feet photos is legal in the US, UK, and most places — feet aren't explicit content, which is a big part of why the door is so open. The details, though, come down to your circumstances.

The conditions that count

  • You have to be 18+ and able to prove it.
  • Sell only what you shot and have the right to distribute.
  • Anyone in frame must be a consenting adult, with any required records kept.
  • Laws, platform rules, and tax treatment differ by country and region.

Staying straight with tax

  • Feet-pic income is generally taxable — even part-time, even with no form issued.
  • In the US, many report on Schedule C, and self-employment tax can kick in once net earnings hit $400.
  • Parking ~25–30% aside is a sensible habit, not an official rule.
  • Keep records from sale one and check the current rules where you live.
